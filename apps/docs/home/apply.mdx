Going live on **mainnet requires approval**. You register your app in the [NEAR Auth Console](https://console.auth.near.org), and once it is approved the console creates your production Auth0 application and shows you its credentials. This page explains what you get and walks you through the process.

<Tip>
  Building locally? You can develop against testnet using the values in [Resources → Testnet](/resources/testnet), then come back here when you're ready to ship on mainnet.
</Tip>

---

## What approval grants you

On **mainnet**, each approved app gets its **own** Auth0 `clientId`, with the domains you registered as its callback URLs and web origins. It can request signing tokens, so your users can sign mainnet transactions.

| Item | What you receive |
|------|------------------|
| **Auth0 credentials** | Your own production `clientId`. The `domain` and `signingAudience` are the mainnet network's defaults |
| **Integration snippet** | The provider config for your app, ready to paste |

## How the approval process works

<Steps>
  <Step title="Sign in to the console" icon="log-in">
    Open [console.auth.near.org](https://console.auth.near.org) and sign in. Your app is registered under the email you sign in with, which has to be verified. Each email can have one app at a time, a rejected one included, so sign in with a different email for each app.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Register your app" icon="send">
    Fill in the form with your app's details. See [what to prepare](#what-to-prepare) below so you can do it in one pass.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review" icon="search">
    The NEAR Auth team reviews your submission. The console shows its status, and we may reach out at your contact email about your use case or expected volume.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Approval" icon="circle-check">
    Once approved, the console creates your Auth0 application and shows you everything you need to go live:

    - **Your production `clientId`**, with the callback URLs, logout URLs and web origins you registered.
    - **A config snippet** to paste into your provider setup.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  Drop your production credentials into your provider config with `network: "mainnet"` — the rest of your integration stays the same.
</Tip>

<Note>
  **Changing an approved app** — editing its details, or adding a domain, is done in the same console. The change goes back to review, and your app keeps working with its current settings until it is approved.
</Note>

## What to prepare

Have these details ready before you open the console so you can submit in a single pass. Each field maps to a setting on your production application.

<ParamField body="Application name" required>
  The name NEAR Auth displays to your users during login and when they approve a transaction. Use the public-facing name you want people to recognize — e.g. *Acme Wallet*.
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="Application description">
  *Optional.* A short summary of what your application does. It appears next to your name on the NEAR Auth login screen, so write it for end users. One or two sentences.
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="Application logo" required>
  A square logo shown alongside your app name on the login and approval screens. Provide a PNG or SVG (recommended 256×256&nbsp;px) served over HTTPS. A recognizable logo helps users trust the request.
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="Expected monthly active users" required>
  Your best estimate of monthly active users (MAU). This helps us plan capacity as your app grows. Choose one bracket: **Less than 1,000**, **1,000–10,000**, **10,000–25,000**, or **more than 25,000**.
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="Callback URLs" required>
  The exact HTTPS URL NEAR Auth is allowed to redirect back to after login and after a signature request. It **must match the `redirectUri` you configure in the SDK**. Any redirect to a URL you have not registered is rejected. Example: `https://app.example.com/callback`.
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="Logout URLs">
  *Optional.* An HTTPS URL users may be returned to after logging out. Only needed if you pass a return URL when calling `logout`; leave it blank otherwise. Example: `https://app.example.com`.
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="Web Origins" required>
  The origin — scheme + domain, no path — where your app is served and from which it calls NEAR Auth. Used to authorize your domain and enforce CORS, so only your app can start the auth flow. Must be HTTPS. Example: `https://app.example.com`.
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="Primary contact email address" required>
  A monitored email for the person we should contact about your application, for example with questions during review or with security notices. The console does not email you when your app is approved or rejected, so check its status there. Example: `dev@example.com`.
</ParamField>

<Note>
  **Multiple environments** — callback URLs, logout URLs and web origins are lists, so you can register your production and staging domains together. Developing locally? You don't need any of this for testnet — use the shared [testnet credentials](/resources/testnet).
</Note>

<Warning>
  **HTTPS is required** for the Callback, Logout, and Web Origin URLs on mainnet. Plain `http://` URLs are rejected.
</Warning>

## Go live on mainnet

Once your app is approved, point your provider at mainnet using the credentials the console gives you.

```ts main.ts
import { JavascriptProvider } from "@fast-auth-near/javascript-provider";

const provider = new JavascriptProvider({
  network: "mainnet",
  clientId: "<your-auth0-client-id>",
  // domain and signingAudience come from the mainnet network defaults.
});
```

Use the mainnet contract account ids when you construct the client:

```ts client.ts
import { FastAuthClient } from "@fast-auth-near/browser-sdk";

const client = new FastAuthClient(provider, connection, {
  fastAuthContractId: "fast-auth.near",
  mpcContractId: "v1.signer",
});
```

<Warning>
  Production requires your own approved credentials. There is no shared mainnet `clientId`, so use the one the console issues you.
</Warning>
